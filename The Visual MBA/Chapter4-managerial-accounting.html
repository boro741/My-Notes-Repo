Managerial Accounting

How Rakesh calculates cost structures, contribution margins, break-even volumes, and budget variances for internal decision-making!
Topic: Managerial Costing Reading time: ~12 min Concepts: 8 Complete
CONCEPT 1

Financial vs. Managerial Accounting

While Financial Accounting is built for external stakeholders (banks, tax authorities, investors) following strict GAAP rules, Managerial Accounting is built for internal managers to make operational decisions (pricing, costing, budgeting) with no mandated rules.

CONCEPT 2

Cost Classification: Fixed vs. Variable Costs

To make smart pricing decisions, Rakesh must separate his costs into two behaviors:

  • Fixed Costs (FC): Total remains constant regardless of sales volume (e.g. Stall Rent = ₹3,000/month).
  • Variable Costs (VC): Total changes in direct proportion to sales volume (e.g. Tea leaves, milk, sugar = ₹4/cup).

CONCEPT 3

Direct vs. Indirect Costs (Overhead)

Direct Costs can be directly traced to a specific unit of product (tea leaves in a cup of chai). Indirect Costs (Overhead) cannot be easily traced to a single unit (cleaning supplies for the stall, electricity bill) and must be allocated.

CONCEPT 4

Contribution Margin

Contribution Margin (CM) = Revenue − Variable Costs. It represents the amount of money leftover from each sale to cover Fixed Costs and generate profit.

CONCEPT 5

Break-Even Analysis & Cost-Volume-Profit (CVP)

The Break-Even Point is the sales volume where Total Revenue exactly equals Total Costs (Net Income = 0):

Break-Even Volume (Units) = Total Fixed Costs ÷ Contribution Margin per Unit

CONCEPT 6

Target Profit Analysis

To calculate how many cups Rakesh must sell to make a specific desired profit (e.g. ₹6,000/month):

Target Sales (Units) = (Fixed Costs + Target Profit) ÷ Contribution Margin per Unit

CONCEPT 7

Job-Order Costing vs. Process Costing

Job-Order Costing is used when products are unique and custom-made (e.g. catering a custom wedding event). Process Costing is used when identical products are mass-produced continuously (e.g. standardized cups of tapri chai).

CONCEPT 8

Budgeting & Variance Analysis

Managers compare actual financial results against their budget plan. The difference is a Variance:

  • Favorable Variance: Actual revenue was higher than budgeted, or actual cost was lower.
  • Unfavorable Variance: Actual cost was higher than budgeted, or actual revenue was lower.
