This whole section teaches one repeating idea: don't ask ChatGPT for content directly. First make it "become" your customer, get the customer's real pain points, and only then ask for the marketing content. Everything below — Facebook ads, YouTube scripts, emails, cold outreach, LinkedIn messages — is just that one idea applied to a different channel.

2. Role-play prompt"Put yourself in the shoes of [that person]. Describe your daily struggles / pain points."
→
3. Generate contentNow ask for the ad, email, script, or message — and tell it to use the pain points above.
→
4. Push it furtherRegenerate, ask for a shorter/friendlier/less-salesy version. First draft is never the final one.

Create your Go-To-Market Outline with ChatGPT

A go-to-market plan is just your roadmap for actually launching the business.

The trick used here

  • Instead of asking a plain question, we ask ChatGPT to play a role — the CEO of our own startup.
  • Give it 3 things: (1) what your product does, (2) what stage you're at (e.g. "I have a working MVP"), (3) what you want next (e.g. "get my first 100 paying customers").
Prompt usedAssume that you're a CEO of a startup creating [an AI assistant for elderly people]. You have a functional [MVP]. The next step is getting paying customers. Outline a brief go-to-market plan to get to the first 100 paying customers.

What ChatGPT gave back (a broad outline)

  • Identify your target market
  • Conduct market research
  • Develop marketing materials (website, social pages, brochures)
  • Find early adopters, offer incentives
  • Leverage partnerships (e.g. senior centers, retirement communities)
  • Attend events (health fairs, senior expos)
  • Gather feedback and refine the strategy
Key takeawayThis is only a high-level outline. The rest of this section takes each bullet point above and goes deep into it — that's literally the syllabus for lectures 39–52.

⭐ Use this Magic Prompt Before Every ChatGPT Conversation

This is the single most important lecture in the section — everything after this reuses it.

Why you need it

  • ChatGPT's own plan said "identify your target market" — you should already have this from earlier lectures (customer segments, ideal customer persona, lean canvas).
  • Different segments feel different pain. Example for an AI assistant for elderly people: caregivers (who buy it), the elderly themselves (who use it), and B2B buyers (retirement homes, clinics) — three completely different messages are needed.
  • The course focuses on just one segment at a time to keep examples simple, but in real life you repeat this process separately for each segment.

The magic prompt

Prompt usedPut yourself in the shoes of a potential user of such a product. Describe your daily struggles that could be solved using this AI assistant in detail. Describe all the pain points.

Notice it says "user" specifically — not the caregiver, not the retirement home manager. Be precise about which person you're asking ChatGPT to become.

ChatGPT's answer (playing the elderly user)Pain points: memory loss, physical challenges, loneliness, safety concerns, communication difficulties.
How the assistant helps: reminders & scheduling, physical assistance, companionship, safety monitoring, communication support.

Now turn those pain points into content

Follow-up promptNow craft a Facebook post copy targeted at this audience based on the previous pain points.
Result"Are you tired of struggling with memory loss, physical challenges and loneliness? Do you worry about your safety and communication difficulties? Our new AI assistant is here to help... Learn more and sign up today."
Golden ruleBefore you ask ChatGPT for any marketing content — a post, a script, an email, anything — run the "put yourself in the shoes of..." prompt first. Content built on real pain points always beats content built on a generic product description.

Find the Best Social Media Channels with ChatGPT

Once ChatGPT is "in character" as your customer, ask it where that customer actually spends time.

Prompt usedList 5 social media channels that would be the most efficient to reach this audience.
  • Facebook — largest platform, has a strong share of older users, precise ad targeting by age/interest.
  • YouTube — older adults increasingly use it.
  • Instagram — mainly younger, but the older-user share is growing.
  • Twitter — listed too, though the instructor was skeptical of this one.
  • LinkedIn — good for elderly people still working, and also useful for reaching B2B partners like retirement homes.
Key takeawayTreat this list as a starting point, not gospel. ChatGPT can be wrong or generic — sanity-check its picks against what you already know about your audience (or quick real research).

Craft a Facebook or Instagram Post with ChatGPT

Step 1 — ask for the ad copy

Prompt usedNow create an ad copy for Facebook for this product based on the pain points you described.

Step 2 — ask for a separate headline

Prompt usedWrite a headline for such a Facebook ad.
  • Don't like the result? Click Regenerate response — ChatGPT keeps every version so you can flip left/right and compare.
  • Still not targeted enough? Push it further: "rewrite the headline and clearly target elderly people who experience these struggles."

What about images?

Visuals for the post are covered later in the course using AI image generation — ChatGPT can even help you write the image-generation prompt. This lecture stays text-only on purpose.

Key takeawaySplit the work into small requests — ad body, then headline, then a rewrite — instead of one giant prompt. Small, iterative asks give you more control and better results.

Design an Email Marketing Campaign with ChatGPT

Step 1 — brainstorm email types, not just product emails

A real email campaign gives value, not constant selling. So first ask for a wide list of ideas:

Prompt usedList 20 ideas of emails that could be sent and that would grab the attention of this audience. The emails don't need to only focus on the product.
  • Welcome email (first email to new subscribers)
  • Pain-point series (one email per pain point)
  • Customer success stories
  • Product demo video
  • Customer testimonials
  • Educational content (e.g. memory tips, mobility, safety)
  • Holiday / seasonal greetings
  • Community events

Step 2 — write one specific email

Prompt usedWrite one email for #6 — educational content. Include the email title and the body.
ResultTitle: "Tips for Maintaining Memory as You Age." Body opens with "Dear [subscriber's first name]..." then lists: exercise, stay socially active, challenge your brain, get enough sleep, eat a healthy diet.
Key takeawayYou can keep pushing: "make it longer," "make it more intriguing," or "turn this into a 2-email series, send the second one a few days later." Build the whole campaign this way — ideas first, then write each one, then chain them into a sequence.

Putting It All Together: Design a Sales Funnel with ChatGPT

This lecture connects everything from lectures 40–46 into one structured journey.

Step 1 — ask broadly first

Prompt usedDefine the steps of an online sales funnel for [the product described above].
Result (too vague on its own)Awareness → Interest → Consideration → Purchase → Post-purchase.

Step 2 — ask again, but demand specifics

Prompt usedGive me a precise example of a sales funnel and list all the steps, including which social media and marketing strategies to use.
  • Awareness — Facebook ad campaign targeting people 65+ interested in senior living, caregiving, or elder tech.
  • Interest — click-through to a landing page with testimonials, features, and a free demo signup.
  • Consideration — a follow-up email series building trust and positioning the product as helpful.
  • Purchase — simple checkout, clear pricing, satisfaction guarantee, plus retargeting ads for people who didn't convert.
Key takeawayEvery stage of this funnel is just a channel you already learned in lectures 40–46, now arranged into one path. A different audience segment (like a B2B buyer) needs its own, different funnel — don't reuse the same one for everyone.

ChatGPT for B2B — Generate a List of Business Leads

Use this whole track if your product sells to businesses too (or only to businesses), not just individual consumers.

Step 1 — what types of B2B customers exist?

Prompt usedAssume that you're the CEO of a startup creating an AI assistant for elderly people. You have a functional MVP. The next step is getting B2B customers. List potential B2B customers that could be interested in such a product.
ResultRetirement homes, home health care agencies, insurance companies, hospitals and clinics, pharmacies, non-profit organizations, government agencies, senior living communities.

Step 2 — turn ChatGPT into "Google on steroids"

Prompt usedList 20 existing retirement homes or retirement home chains that currently operate in the US.

This gives you real, named companies to reach out to instead of manually searching Google — just swap in the type of customer and the country you want.

Step 3 — how to reach them, and where they gather

Prompt usedWhat is the best way or channel to reach such customers and promote our product to them?
  • Trade shows and conferences
  • Online advertising
  • Email marketing
  • Referral programs
  • Partnerships
  • Direct mail
  • Content marketing
  • Social media
Follow-upList 10 trade shows and conferences like this in the US.
Key takeawayChatGPT can make mistakes, especially with names and facts — always double-check that the companies and events it lists are real before you rely on them (the instructor spot-checked a few and they did exist).

The Magic Prompt for B2B Research

Same technique as lecture 39, applied to a business buyer instead of an individual.

  • Every type of B2B lead has different pain points — a retirement home doesn't feel the same pressure as an insurance company or a hospital. Craft a separate message for each type.
Prompt usedPut yourself in the shoes of the manager of such a retirement home. Describe your daily struggles that could be solved using this AI assistant for elderly people. Describe all the pain points.
Result (playing the retirement home manager)Managing medications, managing appointments, communication with residents, responding to emergencies, monitoring resident health, providing entertainment for residents with limited mobility/cognitive decline, and reducing staff burnout.
Key takeawayOnly once you have these B2B-specific pain points should you move on to writing the cold email or LinkedIn message — same rule as the B2C side, just pointed at a business role instead of a person.

Write a Cold Email with ChatGPT

Prompt usedNow craft a cold outreach email to such a company that would attract their interest to see a demo of our product. Include the title and the body.
First draftSubject: "Introducing our AI assistant for elderly care and enhancing your retirement home's care services." Body: addresses "the manager" directly, lists their pain points (heavy workload, high volume of requests), then presents the solution and asks for a brief demo.

The instructor liked the body but felt the subject line was too generic/salesy — so he pushed further:

Iteration 1Write another subject, more relatable, less traditional.
Result"Improve care services in your retirement home with our assistant for elderly care."
Iteration 2A less salesy subject.
Result"Enhancing elderly care services at [Retirement Home Name]." — calling out the company's actual name makes it feel personal and far less like a mass email.
Key takeawayDefine the customer → get their pain points → get a first draft → then keep asking for variations ("less salesy," "more relatable," "add their name") until the subject line actually stands out.

Write a LinkedIn Post or Outreach Message with ChatGPT

LinkedIn post

Prompt usedWrite a LinkedIn post to promote our product to such retirement homes.

First draft ran too long, so: "make it shorter." Result opened boldly: "Attention, retirement home managers!"

LinkedIn direct message

Prompt usedCraft a LinkedIn message that we would send to managers of such retirement homes. Grab their attention, but don't appear too salesy. We want to show them a demo of our product.

The first result still felt like a typical, generic LinkedIn pitch. So the instructor kept iterating:

  • "Rewrite it with a more friendly tone and not so polished, make it more simple and relatable" — a little better, still fairly standard.
  • "Be more creative and out of the ordinary" — still came out ordinary.
  • "The language should be as if you were writing to a friend" — this finally produced a genuinely casual "Hey [name], how's it going?" opener.
Key takeawayChatGPT defaults to a safe, corporate tone. Getting something that feels human takes deliberate, repeated pushing — and even then, it's on you to add the final layer of real creativity and personality.
