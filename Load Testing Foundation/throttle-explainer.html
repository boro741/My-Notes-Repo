The setup: a time budget, not a task budget

The kernel manages CPU with a stopwatch, not a to‑do list. Every 100ms period, your app is handed a fixed slice of execution time — its quota, set by however many CPU units you configured. It doesn't matter what the work is; only how long it runs.

⏱️

The period

The scheduler checks in every 100 milliseconds. Each check‑in is a fresh window — your quota resets, whether or not you used it all last time.

🎟️

The quota

0.5 vCPU means 50ms of run time per 100ms period. Spend it all before the period ends, and there's nothing left to spend.

04

What the ratio tells you about your server

The live ratio from your simulation is marked below on the standard health bands. In production, always read this next to CPU utilization — the ratio tells you whether the CPU is the ceiling, not just that something is slow.

0–5% healthy 5–20% moderate 20%+ severe

High ratio + high CPU util

Your app is genuinely CPU‑starved. The quota is the real ceiling — more instances won't help as much as more CPU per instance.

Low ratio + high latency

The CPU isn't the bottleneck. Workers are likely blocked on a slow database call, a lock, or garbage collection — look downstream, not at compute.

Simulation models the CFS bandwidth controller: quota resets every 100ms period; exceeding it pauses execution until the next period.
