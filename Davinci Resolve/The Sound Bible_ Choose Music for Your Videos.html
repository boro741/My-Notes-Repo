The Sound Bible

A beginner’s field guide to choosing music, instruments and sound effects for your videos. No sound-design experience needed.

1. The big idea: sound is a language

You learned English by learning letters, then words, then sentences. Sound works the same way. If you can’t choose music with your gut yet, your “sound dictionary” is small. That’s normal, and fixable.

4. Instrument families

About 15 to 20 instruments appear in most soundtracks. Learn their name, look, sound and family. Remember: the same instrument can carry opposite emotions (a violin can be horror or heartbreak). Context decides.

6. Time and place

Music can say “when” and “where,” like costume and set design do. Ask: do I want to transport viewers there, or bring that world to the present?

7. YouTube creator toolkit

For a vlog or tutorial, use music in layers and by video section. Never just one track from start to end.

8. Mixing basics

Rough starting points for talking videos. Always trust your ears and test on headphones and phone speakers.

  • Voice first. If viewers must strain to hear you, the music is too loud.
  • Duck the music under speech, then bring it up in pauses and b-roll.
  • Silence is a tool. A dropped-out track right before a reveal hits harder than any riser.
  • Cut on the beat or on a downbeat when you change scenes.
  • Fade endings. Don’t chop music mid-note unless it is a deliberate stop.
  • Loudness: YouTube plays videos at roughly -14 LUFS, so aim near it rather than maxing out.
