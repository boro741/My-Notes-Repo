The Vlog Shot Playbook

What clips to capture, what each one is called, and why your editor (future you) will thank you. Written for beginners shooting entertainment vlogs on a phone or a simple camera.

1. The big idea: shots are your vocabulary

Watch a cricket match on TV. The director shows the whole stadium (so you know where you are), then the bowler running in, then the batsman’s face, then the ball, then the crowd reacting, then a replay. Nobody tells you “this is a wide shot” but you understood the whole story. Your vlog works the same way. A good vlogger doesn’t film one long clip of a place. They capture a set of different shots and the edit stitches them into a story.

3. Camera angles

Angle is where the camera sits relative to your subject. It changes how the viewer feels about what they see.

4. Camera movement

Movement adds energy but makes shaky footage look amateur if it’s accidental. Move on purpose, or hold still.

The 5-shot rule for any scene

If you remember nothing else, get these five for every new place or moment: wide (where), medium (who and what they’re doing), close-up (face or key object), detail (small interesting thing) and a reaction. In the edit you’ll always have something to cut to.

7. Composition basics (try it)

Placement matters as much as shot size. Move your subject around the frame and see what each choice does.

8. How much should I shoot?

New vloggers either film too little (nothing to cut to) or too much (hours of useless footage). These are rough starting numbers, not rules.
