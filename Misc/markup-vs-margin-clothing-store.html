The difference in one minute

Most new shop owners use the words markup and margin as if they mean the same thing. They don't, and mixing them up quietly eats your profit, especially when customers start asking for discount.

Start with one shirt. Supplier ne aapko shirt ₹1,000 mein di. Aapne tag ₹1,500 ka lagaya. Customer ne ₹1,500 pay kiye. Gross profit: ₹500.

Markup

A pricing tool. "Maine shirt ₹1,000 mein kharidi. Ab customer ko kitne mein bechun?" You start from the cost.

Margin

A performance tool. "Customer ne mujhe ₹1,500 diye. Usmein se kitna percent mera gross profit hai?" You start from the sale.

Markup: how to price

Markup batata hai ki aapne cost price ke upar kitna extra add kiya. Jab supplier ka bill haath mein ho aur tag lagana ho, tab markup natural tool hai, kyunki cost aapko already pata hai.

Markup % = SP − CPCP × 100

(1,500 − 1,000) ÷ 1,000 × 100 = 50%

Aur price nikalna ho toh formula ulta chalta hai:

Selling price = CP × (1 + Markup%)

₹1,000 × 1.50 = ₹1,500

Margin: how to judge profit

Margin batata hai ki customer ke diye hue paise mein se kitna percent aapke paas profit ke roop mein bacha. Month-end par jab aap register dekhte ho, tab yahi number sach batata hai.

Margin % = SP − CPSP × 100

(1,500 − 1,000) ÷ 1,500 × 100 = 33.33%

Same shirt, same ₹500, lekin 50% markup aur 33.33% margin. Farq sirf denominator ka hai: markup cost se divide karta hai, margin selling price se.

The classic mistake"Mujhe 40% margin chahiye, toh cost pe 40% add kar deta hoon." Galat. 40% markup se sirf 28.57% margin milta hai. Neeche wali table is gap ko fix karti hai.

Why margin pays the rent

Ek simple baat se shuru karte hain: rent, salary aur electricity tab bhi dene padte hain jab ek bhi customer shop mein na aaye. Maan lo yeh fixed expenses ₹60,000 per month hain: rent ₹20,000, salaries ₹25,000, electricity ₹5,000 aur other ₹10,000.

Yeh ₹60,000 kahan se aayenge? Sirf gross profit se, yaani sale ke paise minus clothes ki cost. Aur margin batata hai ki har sale mein se kitna paisa is "rent pool" mein jaata hai. Chalo step by step dekhte hain.

  1. Every ₹100 sale mein se kitna aapka hai?Margin ka matlab hi yeh hai. 20% margin par ₹100 ki sale mein se ₹80 supplier ke liye gaye aur ₹20 aapke paas bache. 40% margin par ₹60 gaye aur ₹40 bache.
  2. Un bache hue paison se rent pay hota haiRent, salary aur electricity un ₹20 ya ₹40 se hi nikalte hain, supplier wale ₹80 ya ₹60 se nahi.
  3. Ab ginti karo: kitni sale chahiye?₹60,000 pay karne hain. Agar har ₹100 se ₹20 bachte hain, toh ₹60,000 ÷ ₹20 = 3,000 baar ₹100, yaani ₹3,00,000 ki sales. Agar ₹40 bachte hain, toh ₹60,000 ÷ ₹40 = 1,500 baar ₹100, yaani ₹1,50,000.

The break-even formula, demonstrated

Upar ki ginti ko hi ek formula bana dete hain. Break-even ka matlab hai woh sales jahan gross profit exactly expenses ke barabar ho jaye. Na profit, na loss.

Break-even sales = Monthly expensesGross margin (as a decimal)

Margin ko decimal mein likho: 20% = 0.20, 40% = 0.40.

Example ke saath, 20% margin par:

₹60,000 ÷ 0.20 = ₹3,00,000

Check karo: ₹3,00,000 × 20% = ₹60,000 gross profit. Expenses bhi ₹60,000. Left over ₹0. Yahi break-even hai.

Gold aur green milkar gross profit hain. Gold hissa expenses pay karta hai, green hissa aapke paas bachta hai. Is example mein same sales par 40% margin wali shop ke paas ₹60,000 extra hain. (Yeh final net profit nahi hai, tax aur interest abhi baaki hain.)

In shirts, not rupees

₹1,500 ki shirt par 33.33% margin ka matlab hai ₹500 gross profit per shirt. Toh ₹60,000 ÷ ₹500 = 120 shirts per month, yaani roz lagbhag 4 shirts sirf expenses cover karne ke liye.

What if you also want profit?

Break-even sirf zero-profit point hai. Apne liye kuch kamana ho toh desired profit ko expenses mein add kar do:

Sales needed = Expenses + Desired profitGross margin

₹30,000 profit chahiye, margin 40%: (60,000 + 30,000) ÷ 0.40 = ₹2,25,000

The pointHigher margin ka matlab hai ki rent pay karne ke liye kam sales chahiye. Chhote town mein jahan footfall limited hai, yeh bahut bada fayda hai.

How much margin should you aim for?

Koi ek "sahi" margin nahi hota. Aapko teen numbers nikalne hain: minimum margin (neeche nahi jaana), target margin (jo aap chahte ho) aur maximum margin (jahan market rok deta hai). Pehle do aapke apne numbers se nikalte hain, teesra market se.

1. Minimum margin: the survival line

Yeh woh margin hai jahan gross profit sirf expenses cover kare. Isse neeche jaoge toh har mahine loss.

Minimum margin = Monthly expensesExpected monthly sales

Expenses ₹60,000, expected sales ₹2,50,000: 60,000 ÷ 2,50,000 = 24%

"Expected sales" mein realistic number lo, apne normal slow months ke hisaab se. Best festive month ka number lene se margin kam dikhega aur dhokha ho jayega.

2. Target margin: survival plus your own income

Aap shop sirf rent bharne ke liye nahi chala rahe. Apni salary ya profit bhi add karo. Unsold stock aur markdown ka andaza bhi expenses mein jod do.

Target margin = Expenses + Desired profitExpected monthly sales

₹30,000 profit chahiye: (60,000 + 30,000) ÷ 2,50,000 = 36%

Dekho expected sales badalne par kya hota hai (expenses ₹60,000, desired profit ₹30,000):

Expected monthly salesMinimum marginTarget margin
₹2,00,00030%45%
₹2,50,00024%36%
₹3,00,00020%30%

Sales kam hongi toh aapko zyada margin chahiye. Isliye shuruat mein, jab customers kam hote hain, margin ko lightly mat lo.

3. Maximum margin: the market sets the ceiling

Maximum ka koi formula nahi hota, kyunki yeh customers aur competitors decide karte hain. Aapko pata karna hai ki customers us item ke liye zyada se zyada kitna normally pay karte hain. Nearby shops mein 4 ya 5 similar items ke prices note karo, aur ek typical price lo (sabse mehenga nahi).

Maximum margin = Market price − Your costMarket price

Kurti ki cost ₹600, nearby shops mein similar kurti ₹1,000: (1,000 − 600) ÷ 1,000 = 40%

Isse upar price rakhoge toh customers doosri shop chale jayenge, jab tak aap kuch extra (better fit, quality, alteration, service) na do. Basic items par ceiling kam hoti hai, festive aur wedding wear par zyada.

How to use the three numbers together

  • Minimum ≤ Target ≤ Maximum: good itemAap kahin bhi target price aur market ceiling ke beech price rakh sakte ho. Target ke paas rakhoge toh jaldi bikega, ceiling ke paas rakhoge toh zyada kamaoge.
  • Maximum is below your target, but above minimumItem expenses cover karta hai lekin aapka profit goal nahi. Ceiling par price karo aur volume badhao.
  • Maximum is below minimum: skip itIs item par market itna nahi deta ki woh apna kharcha nikaale. Supplier se cost negotiate karo, ya chhodo, ya jaanbujhkar footfall item ki tarah rakho.

Dhyan rakho: minimum aur target margin poori shop ka average hain. Kuch items ko zyada margin par rakhna padega taaki doosre kam margin wale items sambhal jayein.

Don't forget the bargaining buffer

Agar customers aam taur par discount maangte hain, toh tag price target price se thoda upar rakho:

Tag price = Target selling price1 − Usual discount

Target ₹1,333, customers usually 10% maangte hain: 1,333 ÷ 0.90 = ₹1,481, yaani tag ₹1,499. 10% off ke baad bhi ₹1,349 milta hai.

Bargaining and the floor price

"Bhaiya, thoda kam karo" aapko roz sunna padega. Dekho ek chhota discount kitna bada asar karta hai. Jeans ki cost ₹1,000 hai aur tag ₹1,600. Customer ₹200 kam maangta hai aur aap maan jaate ho.

Before discountAfter discount
Cost price₹1,000₹1,000
Selling price₹1,600₹1,400
Gross profit₹600₹400
Gross margin37.5%28.57%

Customer ko sirf 12.5% discount mila, lekin aapka profit 33.33% gir gaya. Isliye discount ko hamesha profit ke terms mein sochiye, tag price ke terms mein nahi.

Give every item a floor price

Upar nikala hua minimum margin use karo (example ke liye hum 25% lete hain). Phir sabse neeche ka acceptable price nikalo:

Floor price = CP1 − Minimum margin

1,000 ÷ 0.75 = ₹1,333

Floor price for the ₹1,600 jeans₹1,333Maximum discount: ₹267, about 16.7%

Floor price salesman ko bata do, ya tag ke peeche code mein likh do. Phir woh bina loss ke confidently bargain kar sakta hai.

Habits for a first-time store owner

Pehli baar shop chala rahe ho, toh sab kuch ek saath perfect karna zaroori nahi. In habits ko ek-ek karke apni routine mein daalo. Pehle chaar groups mein se jo sabse zyada zaroori lage, wahan se shuru karo.

Pricing and margin

  • Different categories, different marginsBasics jaise innerwear aur plain tees lower margin par rakho taaki footfall aaye. Festive aur wedding-season wear par higher margin rakho.
  • Track margin by itemFast-moving item at 25% margin kabhi kabhi slow item at 50% se zyada kamata hai, kyunki stock jaldi rotate hota hai.
  • Compete on value, not only priceCompetitors ke saath price war mat karo. Fit, quality, alteration aur service se alag dikho.
  • Calculate margin without GSTAgar aap GST registered ho, toh cost aur selling price dono se GST hata ke margin nikalo, warna margin galat dikhega. Exact rules apne CA se confirm kar lo.
  • Negotiate with your supplierCost kam hoti hai toh margin seedha badhta hai. ₹1,500 ke tag par cost ₹1,000 se ₹950 hui toh margin 33.3% se 36.7% ho jaata hai. Bulk order, advance payment ya regular orders par better rate maango.

Buying and stock

  • Start small and reorder the winnersPehli baar mein zyada stock mat lo. Chhoti quantity mein size aur colour ka mix lo, dekho kya bikta hai, aur phir sirf winners reorder karo.
  • Buy for your town's budgetApna taste nahi, apne customers ka budget aur taste dekho. Shuru mein 2 ya 3 price bands (jaise ₹500 ke neeche, ₹500 se ₹1,000, ₹1,000 se upar) rakho aur dekho kaunsa band sabse zyada bikta hai.
  • Remember the hidden costUnsold stock mein aapka paisa phasa hota hai. Jitna zyada time stock pada rehta hai, utna aapka cash block rehta hai.
  • Set a markdown rule in advancePehle se decide karo ki item kitne din mein nahi bika toh discount lagega, jaise 60 din. Rule ready hoga toh decision lena emotionally aasan hota hai.
  • Track stock by size and colourJab sirf XL aur XXL bache hon, toh item slow ho jaata hai. Size-wise count rakho aur reorder karte waqt missing sizes pehle bharo.

Money and records

  • Keep a simple registerHar item ka CP, SP aur margin likho. Notebook ya Excel, kuch bhi chalega.
  • Close the cash every dayRoz shop band karte waqt likho: opening cash, total sales, expenses aur closing cash. Agar kahin gap mile toh usi din check karo.
  • Separate shop money from personal moneyAlag bank account rakho, ya kam se kam alag notebook. Apne liye ek fixed monthly salary decide karo aur baaki paisa business mein rehne do.
  • Keep a cash cushionShuruat ke mahine slow ho sakte hain. Kam se kam 2 mahine ke expenses alag rakho (₹60,000 wale example mein ₹1,20,000) taaki rent ka tension na ho.
  • Count the small costsPacking bags, transport, alteration, repairs aur payment charges. Yeh chhote kharche expenses mein add karo, warna aapka minimum margin kam nikalega.

Customers and daily routine

  • Sell on the floor yourself at firstPehle kuch mahine khud customers ko attend karo. Aapko pata chalega ki log kya poochte hain, kya reject karte hain aur kis price par haan bolte hain.
  • Write down your return and alteration policyPehle se decide karo ki exchange hoga ya nahi, kitne din mein, aur bill ke saath. Customer aur staff dono ko clarity milti hai, aur jhagde kam hote hain.
  • Keep customer contacts, with permissionNaam aur phone number lo, aur festival se pehle new stock ya offer ka message bhejo. Repeat customer sabse sasta customer hota hai.
  • Plan for festive and wedding season earlyEid, Durga Puja, Bihu, Diwali aur wedding season ke liye 6 se 8 hafte pehle stock plan karo, kyunki suppliers ke paas bhi us time rush hota hai.
  • Train staff on the floor priceStaff ko batao ki woh kitna discount de sakte hain aur kab aapse poochna hai. Phir har bargain ke liye aapko shop mein rukna nahi padega.
  • Do a 30-minute weekly reviewHar hafte ek fixed time par dekho: top 5 selling items, 5 slowest items, is hafte ka margin aur cash position. Chhoti problems bade hone se pehle pakad mein aa jaati hain.
  • Check nearby shops every monthMahine mein ek baar nearby shops ke prices aur new designs dekho. Yeh aapke maximum margin ka sabse reliable data hai.
  • Keep the shop neat and easy to browseBest sellers entrance ke paas, size-wise arrangement, saaf trial room aur achhi lighting. Yeh almost free hai aur customer ko rukne ki wajah deta hai.

Quick test

Ek kurti ₹500 mein kharidi aur ₹1,000 mein bechi. Pehle khud socho, phir answer kholo.

Purchase price ke upar kitna extra add kiya? Markup ya margin?

Markup. (1,000 − 500) ÷ 500 = 100% markup.

Customer ke ₹1,000 mein se kitna percent gross profit hai?

Margin. (1,000 − 500) ÷ 1,000 = 50% margin.

100% markup ka matlab 100% margin hota hai kya?

Nahi. 100% markup sirf 50% margin hai. Margin kabhi 100% nahi hota jab tak cost zero na ho.

What to remember

Markup sets the tag.

Margin protects the profit.

A floor price ends the bargaining loss.

Rules of thumb

Always

  • Set the price with markup, judge the profit with margin.
  • Decide a floor price for every item before it goes on the rack.
  • Calculate minimum margin with sales from a slow month.
  • Record cost, selling price and margin for every item.
  • Keep at least 2 months of expenses as a cash cushion.
  • If you are GST registered, calculate margin on prices without GST.

Never

  • Add a margin percentage to cost as if it were markup. 40% markup is only 28.57% margin.
  • Sell below the floor price, except in a planned clearance.
  • Treat gross margin as net profit. Tax, interest and other costs still come out.
  • Price only by copying competitors. Check your own cost first.
  • Mix shop money with personal money.

Checklists

For every new item

  • Write down the cost price.
  • Choose the target margin and calculate the price.
  • Check the nearby market price (the maximum margin).
  • Add the bargaining buffer to get the tag price.
  • Note the floor price in your register.

Every day

  • Close the cash: opening, sales, expenses, closing.
  • Record sales and any expense.
  • Note what customers asked for but you did not have.
  • Tidy the display and put best sellers up front.

Every week (30 minutes)

  • List the top 5 and the slowest 5 items.
  • Calculate the week's gross margin.
  • Check cash against the expenses due.
  • Plan reorders, starting with missing sizes.

Every month

  • Compare gross margin with your target margin.
  • Compare sales with break-even sales.
  • Mark down items that passed your markdown deadline.
  • Review supplier rates and nearby shop prices.
  • Pay yourself the fixed salary you decided on.
